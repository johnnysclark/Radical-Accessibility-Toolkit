# PROGRAM_NOTES — NEH DHAG Requirements (Tier II)

Spec sheet distilled from NEH's DHAG landing page and the most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO). Every section we draft must satisfy something here. Cite back to this file when defending a structural choice.

## Program purpose

DHAG funds innovative, experimental digital humanities projects that contribute to critical infrastructure for scholarly research, teaching, and public programming in the humanities. The program is jointly administered by NEH and IMLS and supports work at multiple stages of maturity.

## Tiers

| Tier | Award ceiling | Stage of maturity |
|------|---------------|-------------------|
| I    | up to $75,000 | Early-stage experimentation, methodology refinement, documenting learning. |
| **II** | **$75,001–$150,000** | **Mid-stage development: refinement, evaluation, broader testing.** |
| III  | $150,001–$350,000 (+ up to $100K federal matching at 1:1 = $450K total) | Mature scaling, sustainability, broader uptake. |

**We are targeting Tier II.** Justification: the Harness is a working prototype deployed with one BLV student over four semesters and already documented in an ACADIA paper; we need refinement, formalization of the tactile-graphic standards, transferability evaluation at partner sites, and a public release — not yet broad scaling.

## Eligibility

- US 501(c)(3) nonprofits, accredited US institutions of higher education (public or private nonprofit), state/local government agencies, federally recognized Native American tribal governments.
- Individuals **cannot** apply directly.
- **UIUC (University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign) qualifies** as an accredited IHE.
- Project staff need not be US citizens or residents.

## Required narrative sections

The narrative is the heart of the proposal. Standard required sections (verify exact names against the current NOFO when finalizing):

1. **Overview of the Project** — concise, non-specialist statement of the main goal, key activities, audiences, expected outputs, and the tier requested.
2. **Statement of Significance and Impact** — argument for how the project benefits humanities disciplines and addresses a need for specific audiences.
3. **Environmental Scan** — survey of related work in the field and how this project differs.
4. **History, Scope, and Duration / Work Plan** — what has been done, what will be done, by when.
5. **Staff** — qualifications and time commitments of PI, co-Is, key personnel.
6. **Final Products and Dissemination** — what the project will produce and how those products will be made publicly accessible.

**Narrative page limits** vary by year and tier. Recent guidance: Tier II narratives are limited (commonly around 10 single-spaced pages, with Tier I shorter and Tier III at 8 pages historically — verify against the current NOFO). The `OUTLINE.md` page-budget assumes 10 pages for Tier II and will be re-budgeted once the current NOFO is confirmed.

## Required supporting documents

- **Budget** (legal document; reviewed for reasonableness against the work plan) + **Budget Justification**.
- **Biographical sketches / CVs** for the project director and key staff.
- **Letters of Commitment** — required for uncompensated participants; optional for compensated staff.
- **Data Management Plan** — max 2 pages; tailored to project outputs, covering formats, dissemination, access, privacy, IP.
- **Work Plan / Timeline** — activities, personnel, schedule.

## Cost share / matching

- **Not required** for Tier I or Tier II.
- Tier III only: optional matching-funds slot up to $100K federal at 1:1 cash match.

## Deadlines

DHAG runs on a **semi-annual cycle** with deadlines in **January** and **May**. The May cycle moved from June in a recent revision. For the May 2026 cycle, project start dates fall between January 1 and March 1, 2026. The next deadline relevant to us should be verified directly against the NEH website / Grants.gov before we lock a submission target.

## Review criteria (in order of emphasis)

Reviewers apply these holistically; specific weights are not published.

1. **Intellectual Significance and Impact** — how the project advances humanities research, teaching, and public engagement.
2. **Feasibility** — timing, methodologies, technologies, team qualifications, identified risks, **and accessibility considerations**.
3. **Clarity of Expression** — proposal quality and organization.
4. **Design and User Experience** — when relevant, evidence of UX testing and user feedback.
5. **Budget and Cost Reasonableness** — alignment of funds with project needs.
6. **Work Plan and Sustainability** — viability of activities and long-term sustainability strategy.

## Accessibility / disability priorities (explicit NEH program value)

DHAG guidance has been strengthened around **accessibility and usability**. Reviewers explicitly appreciate proposals where:

- Project teams include members of target audiences (e.g., users with disabilities).
- Funds are explicitly dedicated to accessible design.
- The team demonstrates commitment to making grant products broadly accessible to people with disabilities.

This is unusually well-aligned with our project. We should foreground — not bury — Daniel Bein's role as co-designer, the explicit accessibility budget, the open-source release strategy, and the structural (not retrofit) framing.

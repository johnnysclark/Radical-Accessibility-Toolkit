# Testimonial — Daniel Bein

Draft for Daniel to personalize, edit, or rewrite entirely. The single most powerful artifact in the packet; nothing the team writes about the project is as persuasive as Daniel writing about his own work. The version that ships is whatever Daniel says it should be.

## Why this matters

Outreach without a user testimonial reads like a research grant proposal. Outreach with one reads like a person. Anthropic gets the first kind of email constantly. They get the second kind rarely.

## Ground rules

- Daniel writes it. We do not put words in his mouth.
- Daniel approves every quote, in writing, before any version of the pitch leaves the team.
- Daniel decides whether his full name appears, whether he's identified by photo or only by name, and whether his testimonial is attached to public pitches or kept private for warm conversations only.
- If Daniel prefers not to provide a testimonial at all, the pitch ships without one. The work is his; the choice is his.

## Draft prompts (for Daniel)

A few question prompts to get past the blank page. Use whichever feel honest; ignore the rest.

1. Before this project, how did you design buildings? What didn't work?
2. Walk through what a normal afternoon of designing looks like now. What's the part you couldn't do before?
3. What is the worst thing you'd lose if this project stopped tomorrow?
4. What do you wish a sighted designer understood about how you work?
5. Has Claude changed anything about how you think about design, or only how you execute it?

## Skeleton (placeholder text — Daniel to overwrite)

> [Opening — one or two sentences about who Daniel is and the moment he realized he needed something different. In Daniel's voice.]
>
> [Middle — a specific concrete thing he did this week using the toolkit. A command he ran. A drawing he printed. A review he gave. Detail is more persuasive than adjective.]
>
> [Closing — what he wants people building AI tools to understand about access. One sentence.]
>
> — Daniel Bein, graduate student, UIUC School of Architecture

## Format notes for the final version

- Length: 80–150 words. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to fit on the one-pager.
- No jargon Daniel wouldn't use in conversation. If a sentence reads like the README, rewrite it until it sounds like Daniel.
- First person throughout. Past tense for "before"; present tense for "now."
- A single specific detail (a command, an artifact, a moment) beats a paragraph of generalities.

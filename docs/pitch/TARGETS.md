# Targets — People and Organizations to Grow RAP

Internal team doc. Not for external distribution.

This is the long list. Use it to seed outreach plans, identify warm-intro paths, and decide which channels to pursue in parallel with the Anthropic pitch (see `STRATEGY.md`). Synthesized from four research passes covering federal funding, private foundations, individual donors, tech companies, hardware partners, disability and architecture organizations, peer academic labs, and international funders.

**Legend**

- **[F]** — potential funder
- **[CA]** — potential co-applicant on a federal grant
- **[A]** — amplifier / endorser / convener; opens doors but rarely writes checks
- **[P]** — product partner (hardware, software, infrastructure)

**Knowledge cutoff caveat.** Research is current through approximately January 2026. Several programs are mid-rebrand, in transition, or have shifted scope; specific entries are flagged accordingly. **Verify every program against its current public RFP page before drafting a proposal.** Dollar ranges are typical, not promised.

**One housekeeping discrepancy.** The ACADIA 2026 paper describes the primary co-designer as a *legally blind undergraduate architecture student*; our earlier pitch docs (one-pager, capability summary, FAQ) say *graduate student*. Reconcile before any outreach goes out — outsiders will notice.

## The short list — twelve targets to act on first

These are the highest-fit, lowest-friction starts. Roughly ordered by how quickly a single conversation could turn into something real.

1. **Graham Foundation** [F] — Architecture's bread-and-butter research funder. Open application cycle (organizational deadline Feb 25, 2026). $5K–$75K. The credential that opens other doors. *Single easiest "yes" on this entire page.*
2. **NSF CISE / IIS Human-Centered Computing** [F] — Small ~$600K / 3yr, Medium ~$1.2M / 4yr. The natural NSF home for the Accessibility Harness.
3. **NIDILRR Field-Initiated Projects (FIP)** [F] — $200K–$600K / 2–3 years. Tighter scope than DRRP, ideal for a single RAP module (tactile renderer pipeline, or the alt-text engine) as a standalone.
4. **IES Special Education Research — Development & Innovation (CFDA 84.324A)** [F] — $100K–$4M depending on track. Direct fit for the pedagogy side; postsecondary BLV access is in-scope.
5. **NCARB Award for Curricular Innovation** [F] — ~$25K–$50K. The four-semester studio integration is exactly what the award funds.
6. **AIA Upjohn Research Initiative** [F] — ~$30K–$40K. Applied architectural research, fast cycle, sit alongside the Graham application.
7. **Ford Foundation Disability x Tech Fund** [F, with Borealis Philanthropy] — $100K–$1M+ scale. Pledge of ~25% disability-inclusive grantmaking by 2027. Largely invitation-based; the path is a warm intro through an existing Borealis cohort grantee.
8. **Perkins School for the Blind — Howe Innovation Center** [F, CA] — Accelerator + pilot grants $10K–$50K, plus willingness to sit as named partner on larger federal awards. Cold submission accepted.
9. **APH (American Printing House for the Blind) — Monarch partnership** [P, F] — Monarch is the production multi-line tactile pin display; software pipelines for it are exactly what APH's R&D division wants. Possible hardware loans + $25K–$100K co-development.
10. **Dot Inc. (Dot Pad)** [P] — The other production tactile pin-array. Active developer outreach; the README's "high-res tactile pin-array display" future direction is their existing product. Probably hardware + small cash.
11. **NFB Jernigan Institute** [F-small, CA, A] — Strongest U.S. blindness-org partner for federal grant co-application. Modest direct funding; outsized credibility.
12. **UW AccessComputing (Sheryl Burgstahler)** [CA] — Named partner on NSF disability-broadening-participation supplements; the single strongest peer-lab match for federal NSF work.

Everything below is depth.

## Federal funding

### NIDILRR (Administration for Community Living, HHS) [F]

- **Disability and Rehabilitation Research Projects (DRRP)** — $500K–$2M total over 3–5 years. RAP fits priority areas in Employment, Community Living and Participation, or Health and Function (framed as postsecondary access).
- **Field-Initiated Projects (FIP)** — $200K–$600K over 2–3 years; Research and Development tracks. Lower bureaucratic overhead than DRRP; ideal for a defined RAP module.
- **Rehabilitation Engineering Research Centers (RERC)** — $4.75M over 5 years per center. RERCs on Universal Design and the Built Environment or on ICT have historically been the right home for assistive-tech-meets-architecture work. Likely requires partnering with an existing RERC host institution.
- **Advanced Rehabilitation Research Training (ARRT)** — ~$160K/year. Postdoc training; capacity-building rather than direct project funding.
- **SBIR (NIDILRR-administered)** — Phase I ~$150K, Phase II ~$1M. Only relevant if RAP spins out a commercial entity. *Verify current status.*

### NSF [F]

- **CISE / IIS Human-Centered Computing (HCC)** — Small ~$600K/3yr, Medium ~$1.2M/4yr. **The single best NSF home for the Accessibility Harness.**
- **EDU directorate — IUSE (Improving Undergraduate STEM Education)** — $300K–$2M. The pedagogy strand fits IUSE's Engaged Student Learning track. Architecture sits at the edge of NSF's STEM definition; pitch carefully.
- **AccessComputing / INCLUDES Alliances** — NSF INCLUDES Alliance awards fund broadening-participation infrastructure ($10M/5yr). Subaward partnership more realistic than leading.
- **CAREER** — ~$500K–$650K over 5 years. Only relevant for a pre-tenure PI. Possible if a junior faculty hire is built around RAP.
- **Smart and Connected Communities (S&CC)** — $1.5M–$2.5M. Requires framing as community-scale infrastructure; weaker fit.
- **Convergence Accelerator** — Tracks rotate; accessibility/assistive-AI tracks have appeared before. Monitor announcements.

### IES (Institute of Education Sciences) [F]

- **Special Education Research Grants (CFDA 84.324A)** — $100K–$4M by track (Exploration, Development & Innovation, Initial Efficacy, Replication, Scale-Up). Annual cycle, summer deadline. **Direct fit; pursue D&I track first.**
- **Education Research Grants (84.305A)** — Same shape, general-education focus; weaker fit.

### US Department of Education / OSEP [F]

- **Stepping-Up Technology Implementation (84.327S)** — ~$500K–$700K/year over 3–4 years. Funds bringing existing evidence-based tech into special-ed practice. *Strong fit once RAP gathers initial efficacy evidence.*
- **OSEP Personnel Preparation grants** — Could fund TVIs trained on RAP-style tools; tangential.

### NEH / NEA [F]

- **NEH Digital Humanities Advancement Grants (DHAG)** — Three tiers: $50K–$100K, $100K–$350K, up to $700K. Strong fit if RAP is framed around tactile/linguistic representation of architectural heritage and pedagogy, not pure engineering.
- **NEA Design / Research Awards in the Arts** — Design grants $10K–$100K; Research Awards up to ~$150K. NEA has funded accessibility-in-design work before.

### NIH [F]

- **NEI (National Eye Institute)** — R01 ~$250K–$500K/yr, R21 ~$275K/2yr. Vision science and low-vision rehab; RAP's creative-authoring framing is indirect. R21 exploratory or an SBIR via a partner company is the realistic path.
- **NICHD / NCMRR (National Center for Medical Rehabilitation Research)** — Weak fit unless RAP frames toward rehab outcomes.

### Illinois state [F]

- **DCEO innovation/workforce grants** — Rotate annually; have funded inclusive workforce development. *Verify current openings.*
- **ICCB / IBHE accessibility initiatives** — Small ($10K–$100K), institutional-compliance oriented. Useful as match funding.
- **IDHS Division of Rehabilitation Services (DRS)** — Service delivery, not research. Potential for a pilot-partnership if RAP supports DRS clients in postsecondary settings.

### Other federal [F]

- **VA — Rehabilitation R&D Service** — Real but narrow; requires VA-affiliated investigator and veteran framing.
- **DARPA** — Generally a poor fit; defense framing required.
- **CDC NCBDDD (Disability and Health Branch)** — Weak fit.

## Caveats and uncertainty

- **Knowledge cutoff.** Everything here is current to approximately January 2026. Microsoft AI4A is mid-rebrand; Mozilla MOSS capacity has been shrinking; Kessler Foundation has phased out external grantmaking; IBM Research accessibility budget post-Kyndryl is unclear; MacArthur Big Bets winds down in 2026. Verify each program at the source.
- **Specific people named here.** Jeffrey Bigham, Patrick Carrington, Shiri Azenkot, Sheryl Burgstahler, Hiroshi Ishii, Sean Follmer, Robin Brewer, Gregg Vanderheiden, Chieko Asakawa, Bob McNeel — all are publicly cited as field-defining figures in their respective labs/organizations. None of them are "our contact." Confirm current affiliation before listing on any application or in any cold outreach.
- **Donor focus corrections.** Ballmer Group's stated portfolio is *not* primarily disability; Brin's focus is *not* primarily blindness. Both were on our brainstorm list; both have weaker direct fit than the brief implied. Recalibrate before pursuing.
- **Unverified entities.** "Stanford Ability Hub" could not be confirmed as a current named entity. UC Berkeley Disability Lab existed but current status unverified. UMass Subramonyam may be misattributed (likely at Stanford GSE). Confirm before naming.
- **Unsolicited proposals.** Most large family offices (Brin, Scott, Ballmer, Bezos, Hilton) explicitly do not accept them. Cold applications will not work; warm intros via existing grantees, UIUC advancement office, or co-PI networks are the only realistic channel.
- **Architecture vs. STEM at NSF.** NSF EDU programs treat architecture inconsistently; the IUSE pitch needs to lead with the computing / interaction-design content of the Harness, not the building-design output.
- **The undergraduate / graduate-student discrepancy** between the ACADIA paper and the pitch docs needs to be resolved before any outreach. It's the kind of detail a careful reader will catch.
